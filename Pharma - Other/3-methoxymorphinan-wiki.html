1. Identity & structure

3-Methoxymorphinan (3MM; CAS 1531-25-5; PubChem CID 5484286) is a secondary-amine morphinan. It is generated in vivo by CYP3A4-mediated N-demethylation of dextromethorphan (DXM) and of the unmarketed opioid levomethorphan. Because the N-methyl group required for high-affinity classical opioid binding is absent, 3MM possesses a pharmacological profile distinct from both DXM and levorphanol-type opioids.

PubChem 2D depiction (CID 5484286). Aromatic A-ring with 3-methoxy substituent; secondary amine at the nitrogen bridge of the morphinan cage.

PubChem 3D conformer. The rigid tetracyclic morphinan cage and the free secondary amine are the structural features that drive sodium-channel block and α3β4 nAChR interaction while abolishing classical µ-opioid affinity.

2. Pharmacodynamics

  • Opioid receptors — Essentially inactive. Binding affinity is negligible (roughly 30- to >100-fold lower than corresponding 3-hydroxy or N-methyl morphinans). No clinically relevant µ-, κ- or δ-mediated analgesia, euphoria or respiratory depression is expected.
  • Local anesthesia — Primary quantifiable effect: peripheral voltage-gated sodium-channel blockade producing motor, proprioceptive and nociceptive block.
  • α3β4 nAChR — Non-competitive inhibitor; used as an SAR probe in laboratory studies of neuronal nicotinic receptors.
  • Other channels — Related morphinans interact with NMDA and 5-HT3 receptors; 3MM itself shows weaker or absent activity at some of these sites compared with DXM or certain synthetic analogs.

4. Local-anesthetic potency & duration (Hou 2006 & related)

Primary data: Hou et al., Eur. J. Pharmacol. 2006 — sciatic-nerve blockade in rats (motor function, proprioception, nociception). Lidocaine as active control. Follow-on spinal and infiltrative studies confirm the same rank order and the longer duration of the secondary-amine metabolites.

Spinal anesthesia (Chen et al., 2007)
Rank order on ED50 basis shifted slightly: bupivacaine > dextrorphan > 3-methoxymorphinan > dextromethorphan. On an equipotent basis, dextrorphan and bupivacaine produced longer nociceptive blockades than DXM or 3MM. Motor-block durations were more similar across agents.

Co-administration of 3MM (or DXM / DXO) with lidocaine produced additive, not synergistic, blockade of the sciatic nerve.

Step Enzyme Product Normal contribution
O-demethylation CYP2D6 Dextrorphan Dominant first-pass route
N-demethylation CYP3A4 3-Methoxymorphinan Minor under basal conditions
Sequential CYP2D6 / CYP3A4 3-Hydroxymorphinan Downstream of both pathways

Because formation of 3MM is almost exclusively CYP3A4-dependent, the urinary (or plasma) metabolic ratio DXM / 3MM has been validated as a non-invasive probe of CYP3A4 activity (Min et al., 1999 and subsequent single-point methods).

6. Effects of CYP3A4 inducers on the DXM → 3MM axis

Strong CYP3A4 induction does not merely accelerate total DXM clearance; it re-routes a larger fraction of the dose into the N-demethylation pathway.

Inducer Typical exposure DXM AUC / Cmax DXM / 3MM ratio Absolute 3MM formation DXO formation
Rifampin (potent) 600 mg QD × 5–10 d ↓ 70–90 % ↓ (more 3MM relative to parent) Can rise (higher fractional conversion) ↓ (substrate diversion)
Carbamazepine Chronic therapeutic ↓ 40–70 % ↓ Modest increase possible Moderate ↓

Key kinetic point
Even though parent DXM exposure falls sharply, the absolute amount of 3MM formed can increase because the fractional contribution of the CYP3A4 pathway rises markedly. Downstream 3-hydroxymorphinan also increases. The classic DXM/DXO ratio (CYP2D6 probe) becomes unreliable; the DXM/3MM ratio becomes a sensitive marker of the induced state.

7. CYP2D6 phenotype interaction (PM vs EM)

Because 3MM is itself a substrate of CYP2D6 (O-demethylation → 3-hydroxymorphinan), CYP2D6 genotype/phenotype strongly modulates circulating and urinary 3MM levels independently of CYP3A4 induction.

Phenotype Effect on 3MM Consequence
Extensive metabolizer (EM) Rapid conversion of 3MM → 3HM; plasma 3MM often near or below quantification limit after ordinary DXM doses N-demethylation pathway is “hidden” by fast secondary metabolism
Poor metabolizer (PM) Impaired O-demethylation; AUC and urinary recovery of 3MM rise markedly 3MM becomes a more prominent circulating metabolite; contributes to the overall antitussive profile in PMs

In Japanese and French population studies, the percentage of conjugated metabolites (glucuronides of DXO and 3HM) reached 86–99 %. Conjugation itself is not rate-limiting. In PMs the urinary metabolic ratios involving 3MM therefore become especially sensitive read-outs of residual CYP3A4 activity.

Kinetic parameters (rat liver microsomes, illustrative)
O-demethylation of 3MM → 3HM shows large strain differences that model human CYP2D6 polymorphism:
Sprague-Dawley (high activity): Km ≈ 1.8 µM, Vmax ≈ 36 nmol/mg/h, CLint ≈ 21 ml/h/mg
Dark Agouti (low activity): Km ≈ 109 µM, Vmax ≈ 11.5 nmol/mg/h, CLint ≈ 0.11 ml/h/mg
(~180-fold difference in intrinsic clearance).

8. Downstream metabolite: 3-hydroxymorphinan

3-Hydroxymorphinan (3HM) is formed from both DXO (by CYP3A4 N-demethylation) and from 3MM (by CYP2D6 O-demethylation). Unlike its precursors it has attracted independent interest as a potential neuroprotective agent.

  • Potent neurotrophic / neuroprotective effects on LTS- and MPTP-treated dopaminergic neurons of the nigrostriatal pathway.
  • Does not produce the neuropsychotoxic side-effects (dissociation, hallucinations) characteristic of DXO or high-dose DXM.
  • Lacks significant NMDA-receptor binding and anticonvulsant activity; neuroprotection appears to involve suppression of presynaptic voltage-dependent Ca2+ entry and protein-kinase-C activity, reducing glutamate release.
  • Oral bioavailability is low (~18 %); a prodrug (GCC1290K) has been developed and has received FDA NDA clearance for further development as a potential antiparkinsonian agent.

Thus, under conditions that elevate 3MM (CYP3A4 induction + CYP2D6 PM status), a larger fraction of the DXM dose is ultimately converted into a metabolite with a distinctly different therapeutic profile from either parent or primary O-demethylated product.

9. Net clinical picture when DXM is taken with a strong CYP3A4 inducer

  • Classic DXM- and DXO-mediated central effects (σ1/NMDA-linked dissociation, cough suppression, etc.) are attenuated because parent and primary O-demethylated metabolite concentrations fall.
  • Any residual or novel effects shift toward the 3MM profile:
    • longer-lasting peripheral sodium-channel block (if local concentrations become relevant),
    • modest α3β4 nAChR non-competitive inhibition (IC50 ≈ 13.5 µM),
    • negligible opioid activity,
    • possible contribution of elevated downstream 3HM with its own neuroprotective but non-psychotomimetic pharmacology.
  • Systemic local-anesthetic toxicity risk remains low; absolute plasma levels of 3MM are still modest under ordinary oral DXM doses.
  • Urinary recovery of 3MM (and 3HM) rises; the DXM/3MM metabolic ratio falls and can be used to confirm induction.
  • Quantitative induction magnitude: rifampin (300 mg b.i.d. × 7 d) reduced the 0–72 h DXM/3MM urinary metabolic ratio consistent with an approximately 830 % (±1808 %) increase in CYP3A activity (Jones et al., 1996). The effect was independent of CYP2D6 phenotype and visible even in shorter (24–48 h) collections.
Practical implication
Co-administration of a potent CYP3A4 inducer (rifampin, carbamazepine, chronic St John’s wort, etc.) with DXM will blunt the usual psychoactive and antitussive profile of DXM while simultaneously increasing the fractional formation of 3MM and 3HM. In a CYP2D6 poor metabolizer the accumulation of 3MM is amplified further. Clinical monitoring of expected DXM effects (or of CYP3A substrates co-prescribed with the inducer) is warranted.

10. Synthetic intermediate & analytical utility

  • Industrial synthesis — 3MM is a key intermediate in the manufacture of levorphanol and related morphinans. The secondary amine allows selective N-alkylation or other functionalization that is blocked by the N-methyl group of methorphan.
  • CYP3A4 phenotyping probe — The urinary (or single-point plasma) ratio DXM / 3MM remains one of the few non-invasive markers that specifically interrogates the N-demethylation route. It is complementary to the classic DXM/DXO ratio (CYP2D6) and to midazolam or cortisol-based CYP3A probes.
  • Bioanalytical methods — Validated LC-MS/MS assays exist for quantification of (+)-3-methoxymorphinan in human plasma at the low-ng/mL level, supporting both phenotyping studies and forensic/toxicological work.
