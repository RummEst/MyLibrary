Cariprazine vs Aripiprazole

Third-generation D2/D3 partial agonists — full mechanism, PK, clinical, and safety comparison

Both share the 2,3-dichlorophenylpiperazine pharmacophore that anchors dopamine-receptor binding. Cariprazine adds a trans-cyclohexyl dimethylurea tail (drives D3 preference + long half-life); aripiprazole uses a butoxy-dihydroquinolinone tail (drives higher overall bioavailability and IM depot chemistry). 3. Receptor Binding, Occupancy & Intrinsic Activity

Affinity (Ki, nM) = how tightly the drug binds (lower = tighter). Occupancy % = how much of the receptor pool is bound at clinical plasma levels, from PET imaging. Intrinsic activity % = how strongly the drug activates the receptor relative to dopamine/serotonin itself (100% = full agonist, 0% = pure antagonist) — these numbers are assay- and receptor-density-dependent and vary meaningfully across studies; treat as directional, not absolute.

7. Bottom Line

Aripiprazole is D2-preferring, has two decades more real-world use, broader approved indications (autism irritability, Tourette's), and offers long-acting injectable depots — the more flexible, better-characterized agent, with PET data showing it reliably drives D2 occupancy into the 85–96% range at clinical doses without much EPS. Cariprazine's D3-preference is the theoretical basis for claimed benefit on negative symptoms, cognition, and depression, and PET confirms its D3 occupancy saturates faster and at lower doses than its D2 occupancy — but the long DDCAR half-life means side effects and interactions resolve over weeks, not days. This is a pharmacology reference, not prescribing guidance — a prescriber weighs CYP2D6 status, comorbidities, and target symptoms before choosing between them.
